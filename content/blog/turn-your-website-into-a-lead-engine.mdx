A lot of business websites are basically online brochures. They list what the
company does, look reasonably tidy, and then... nothing. No nudge, no next step,
no reason for the visitor to do anything. That's a missed opportunity, because a
website should have a job: turn attention into enquiries.

Here's what tends to make the difference. None of it's complicated.

## Decide what you want people to do

Every page should have one obvious next step. Call, book, buy, get a quote -
pick one and make it impossible to miss. When you cram five competing buttons in
front of someone, they don't pick the best one, they freeze and pick none.

## Build a bit of trust before you ask

People buy from businesses they trust, and a website that's all sell and no proof
doesn't earn it. Easy things that help:

- Real reviews and testimonials from actual customers
- A couple of case studies or examples of work you've done
- Being upfront about pricing, or at least a starting point
- A photo of an actual human - about pages convert better than people think

## Get rid of the friction

Every extra form field, confusing menu and slow-loading page costs you. Ask for
the minimum you need (you don't need their life story to send a quote), make
forms forgiving, and make sure the whole thing works properly on a phone, because
that's where most people will see it.

## Give people a small first step

Not everyone's ready to commit today, and that's fine. Offer a smaller yes - a
free [website health check](/contact?service=Free+Website+Health+Check), a quick
guide, a no-pressure chat. It gets the conversation going without asking for a
wedding ring on the first date.

## Then actually measure it

You can't improve what you don't track. Watch your enquiries, see where people
drop off, change one thing at a time. It's not glamorous, but small, deliberate
tweaks add up.

Get the basics right and your website stops being a line item on your costs and
starts pulling its weight as one of your better salespeople.
