A lot of business websites are really just **online brochures** — they describe
what the company does and then... nothing. They don't guide visitors, build
trust or ask for action. A good website should do a job: turn attention into
enquiries.

## Start with one clear action

Every page should have an obvious next step. What do you want a visitor to do —
call, book, buy, enquire? Decide, then make that action impossible to miss.
Competing calls-to-action just create hesitation.

## Build trust before you ask

People buy from businesses they trust. Make it easy with:

- **Testimonials and reviews** from real customers
- **Case studies** showing results you've delivered
- **Clear pricing or starting points** so there are no nasty surprises
- **A human face** — about pages and photos genuinely convert

## Remove friction

Every extra form field, confusing menu or slow page costs you leads. Ask for
the minimum you need, make forms forgiving, and make sure the whole thing is
fast on a phone.

## Give people a low-commitment first step

Not everyone is ready to buy today. Offer a smaller "yes" — a free
[website health check](/contact?service=Website+Auditing), a guide, or a quick
discovery call. It starts the relationship without pressure.

## Measure, then improve

You can't improve what you don't measure. Track enquiries, see where people drop
off, and make small, deliberate changes. Conversion optimisation is a habit,
not a one-off.

Get these fundamentals right and your website stops being a cost — and starts
being one of your best salespeople.
