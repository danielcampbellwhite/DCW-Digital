If you've spent any time around web development lately, you'll have heard the
name **Vercel**. In a few short years it's gone from a niche tool to one of the
most popular ways to deploy modern websites and apps — and for good reason.
Here's a plain-English look at what it is, why it's everywhere, and why it
matters even if you never touch the code yourself. (Full disclosure: this very
site runs on it.)

## So what is Vercel?

In simple terms, **Vercel is a platform for hosting and deploying websites**.
"Deploying" just means taking the code a developer has written and getting it
live on the internet, fast and reliably.

What makes Vercel stand out is that it was built by the same team behind
**Next.js** — one of the most popular frameworks for building modern websites.
That close relationship means the whole experience is smooth, fast and
designed around how people actually build for the web today.

## Why developers love it

A few things turned Vercel from "another host" into a favourite:

- **Push to deploy.** Connect your code to a Git repository (like GitHub), and
  every time you save changes, your site updates automatically. No fiddly
  manual uploads.
- **Preview deployments.** This is the killer feature. Every change gets its own
  live preview link *before* it goes live — so you can review it, share it with
  a client, and sign it off with confidence.
- **A global edge network.** Your site is served from locations all around the
  world, so it loads quickly wherever your visitors are.
- **Zero fuss.** Sensible defaults mean a lot of the painful configuration
  simply disappears. You can go from code to live site in minutes.

> The first time you share a preview link with a client and they comment on it
> in real time, you understand why people switch.

## It's grown well beyond hosting

Vercel started as a place to put your website, but it's expanded into a proper
**"frontend cloud"**. Alongside hosting, you now get things like serverless
functions (small bits of backend logic that run on demand), built-in
**analytics and performance insights**, easy custom domains, and managed
database integrations. For a lot of projects, it's now a one-stop shop.

## What this means for your business

You might be thinking: *that's all very technical — why should I care?* Fair
question. Here's the business value:

- **Faster, more reliable websites.** Vercel's global network and performance
  focus mean your site loads quickly and stays online — which is good for both
  customers and [search rankings](/blog/why-your-business-website-should-be-fast).
- **Smoother projects.** Preview links mean you can see and approve changes
  before they go live, with no nasty surprises.
- **Room to grow.** The same setup that powers a small business site can scale
  up to a busy web application, so you're not boxed in later.
- **Sensible costs.** There's a genuinely useful free tier, and paid plans are
  predictable — no surprise hosting bills.

## The honest take

Vercel isn't the only option, and it isn't always the right one — a simple
static brochure site or a particular budget might point elsewhere. But for the
kind of fast, modern, scalable websites I build, it's an excellent default that
makes the whole process smoother for everyone involved.

The bigger point is this: the tools we use shape how good — and how stress-free
— the end result is. Choosing the right platform is part of the job, and it's
one of the quiet reasons a well-built site just *feels* better to run.
