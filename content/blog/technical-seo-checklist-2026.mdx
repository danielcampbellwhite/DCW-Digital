You can write the best content in your industry and still be invisible on Google
if the technical foundations are broken. I see it constantly. The good news is
most of it is fixable, and a lot of it is quick.

Here's the checklist I actually work through on an audit. Nothing groundbreaking,
just the stuff that matters and that people keep getting wrong.

## Can Google find and read your pages?

Start here, because if this is broken nothing else matters:

- Your **robots.txt** isn't accidentally blocking pages you want indexed (I've
  seen entire sites blocked by one stray line after a redesign).
- You've got an **XML sitemap**, it's current, and it's submitted in Search Console.
- Important pages return a **200**. Redirects are **301**, not 302, and you're
  not chaining five of them together.
- **Canonical tags** point at the right version of each page so you're not
  competing with yourself.

## On-page basics

- Every page has its own **title** and **meta description**. Not duplicated, not
  auto-generated mush.
- One clear **`<h1>`** per page, with headings that actually describe the content.
- **URLs** that read like words, not `?id=8842&cat=12`.
- **Alt text** on images that mean something.

## Structured data

This is the bit people skip, and it's free wins. Structured data (schema.org)
tells search engines what your content actually is, and it's what gets you those
rich results - star ratings, FAQs, breadcrumbs in the listing.

Worth adding:

- `Organization` or `LocalBusiness` for the brand
- `BreadcrumbList` for navigation
- `Article` / `BlogPosting` on posts
- `Product` and `FAQPage` where they fit

## Speed and mobile

- Passing **Core Web Vitals** on mobile (I wrote about why that matters
  [here](/blog/why-your-business-website-should-be-fast)).
- Genuinely **responsive**, not a desktop site squashed onto a phone.
- **HTTPS** everywhere.

## The bit nobody tells you

Technical SEO isn't a one-and-done job. Sites drift. Someone adds a plugin, a
redirect gets missed, a dev pushes a `noindex` to staging and forgets to remove
it (we've all done it). The sites that hold their rankings are the ones that get
checked now and then, not set up once and forgotten.

That's the whole point of a [website audit](/services#website-auditing) - catch
this stuff before it quietly costs you traffic.
